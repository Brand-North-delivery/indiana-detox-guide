Plain-language definitions

Drug and alcohol treatment

Substance use disorder

A health condition involving a problematic pattern of substance use that causes clinically significant impairment or distress. Diagnosis belongs to qualified clinicians.

Drug rehab

An umbrella term for organized treatment and recovery services addressing problematic drug use. Services may include assessment, withdrawal management, medications, counseling, residential or outpatient care, and continuing support.

Alcohol rehab

Organized treatment and recovery services for alcohol-related problems. Depending on individual need, care can include medically supervised withdrawal, medications, counseling, residential or outpatient treatment, and continuing support.

Medical detox

Clinical evaluation, monitoring, and treatment of intoxication or withdrawal risk. Detox is a stabilization phase; it is not the same as a complete rehabilitation plan.

Residential treatment

A structured program in which a person lives at the facility while receiving services. Residential programs differ in clinical intensity, staffing, and medical capability.

Outpatient treatment

Treatment delivered through scheduled visits without an overnight stay. Frequency, duration, and clinical intensity vary.

Dual diagnosis

Also called co-occurring disorder treatment: integrated or coordinated care for substance-use needs and mental-health conditions occurring together.

Medications for addiction treatment

Use of approved medications as part of treatment for certain substance use disorders, commonly combined with clinical and recovery support based on individual need.

Continuing care

Ongoing treatment and recovery support after an initial phase. It may include outpatient care, medications, peer support, recovery housing, or monitoring.

Definitions are educational summaries informed by SAMHSA's treatment overview and the ASAM Criteria.

Clinical topics

Substances discussed by the guide

Alcohol

Alcohol withdrawal can become medically dangerous. Placement should account for withdrawal history, current use, other substances, and health conditions.

Opioids

Care may include withdrawal support, medications for opioid use disorder, overdose prevention, counseling, and continuing treatment.

Benzodiazepines

Abrupt discontinuation can be dangerous. A qualified clinician should determine whether monitored withdrawal management or a taper is appropriate.

Stimulants

Assessment may need to address sleep, mood, psychosis, cardiovascular symptoms, and co-occurring substance use.

Cannabis

Evaluation considers pattern of use, functional effects, mental health, other substances, and treatment goals.

Polysubstance use

Using more than one substance can change intoxication, withdrawal, overdose, medication, and placement risks.

Fourth Edition continuum

ASAM levels of care

The ASAM Criteria is a person-centered framework for assessing needs, planning treatment, and matching a patient to an appropriate level of addiction care. The Fourth Edition retains four broad levels, with decimal levels describing differences in intensity and capability.

Important: ASAM level is a clinical placement and program-capability concept, not a rating. The guide does not assign any center below to an ASAM level without authoritative, location-specific evidence.
