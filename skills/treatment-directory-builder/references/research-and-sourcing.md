# Research and Sourcing

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest source for each claim: state licensing records, accreditation bodies, official provider pages, Google Business Profile for public NAP/reviews, reputable reporting for context, and aggregators only as leads.

Never infer a clinical service from a category label, marketing phrase, amenity, or review.

## Required research record

For every physical campus capture exact source name; full address; display and E.164 phone; hours; official location and parent URLs; Google profile URL; rating, count, visible star distribution, and capture date; program names with source pages; license/accreditation IDs; media provenance; and conflicts or unknowns.

Store `observedAt`, `sourceUrl`, and `sourceType` with volatile claims. A source URL alone is not a date.

## Scraping and browser collection

Prefer official APIs or structured data where access and terms permit. Otherwise use a normal browser session and visible page state.

- Respect robots directives, terms, rate limits, authentication boundaries, and privacy law.
- Never bypass CAPTCHAs, access controls, paywalls, or anti-bot systems.
- Keep volume low, cache results, and stop after repeated blocking.
- Collect public business facts, never patient identities or sensitive health information.
- Verify one physical location at a time. Confirm heading, street, and city before recording phone or rating.
- Save source URL and timestamp. Use screenshots only for necessary audit evidence when permitted.
- Preserve conflicts. If GBP and admissions phones differ, label both rather than silently choosing one.

## Reviews

Ratings are volatile third-party snapshots, not guide scores.

- Record rating and count with date and live profile link.
- Examine recent, relevant, and lower-rated feedback, not only praise.
- Summarize recurring operational themes; distinguish allegations from facts.
- Convert concerns into direct verification questions.
- Avoid reviewer names, health details, and long quotations.
- Never calculate a clinical ranking from reviews alone.

## Refresh cadence

- Monthly: NAP, hours, rating/count, broken links
- Quarterly: programs, staffing claims, accreditation, insurance language
- Immediately: closure, ownership change, regulatory action, material safety issue, or correction

Record change, reason, source, editor, and date.
